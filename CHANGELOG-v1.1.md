# Walkback 1.1 (in progress, branch `v1.1`)

Version 1.0 is the one submitted to the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026: the release `v1.0-submission` and `main`. Nothing here is in that release. Every number on screen is still the plan B estimate over the Gale relief, marked ESTIMATE.

Status: each item passes the automatic test without the headset (`Walkback > Run Auto Test`). **Not yet seen in the Quest 3.**

## New

- **Stop preview.** Point at a stop's pillar and the side board shows that stop's card, marked "PREVIEW · press the pillar to select it". The selected stop does not change and 360° PHOTO is off while previewing. Leaving the pillar brings the selected stop's card back.
- **Your own stops.** A short tap on bare ground (press and release within 0.45 s, without moving, not while the map is dragged) adds "My stop N" there. It goes where it adds the least straight-line distance to the loop habitat → stops → habitat, and the route, the verdict and the charts are planned again at once. Picked stops have a blue pillar, no science note, no photo and no tasks until the astronaut sets them. On ground steeper than 20° the verdict reads NO SAFE ROUTE. At most 4, never within 20 m of another pillar.
- **CLEAR** on the side board (C on the keyboard) takes the picked stops away and the data's plan comes back. The stop buttons keep the four Curiosity drill sites.
- **Minutes of work at your stops.** -5 MIN / +5 MIN on a picked stop's card, from 0 to 120 min. The line reads "Work: N min · your estimate", and time, oxygen and the verdict follow at once.

- **REMOVE on a picked stop's card**, in the place of 360° PHOTO (a picked stop has no photo): takes that one stop away. CLEAR still takes them all.
- **What the ground says before you tap.** While the pointer is on bare ground of the map, a small card over the dot reads, for example, "14 min back on foot · 8° slope / ESTIMATE · tap to add a stop here". The minutes are the RETURN layer's own (plan B over the relief). Over 20° it reads "too steep to walk", where there is no way back "no way back on foot", and in a WHAT IF "too far with 8 %", all in red.
- **The point of no return.** When the plan does not fit its tank (a WHAT IF, a SUITS reading, or stops added), a red pillar stands at the last point of the route from where the oxygen above the reserve still covers the way home (the RETURN layer's minutes), counting the walk so far and the work at each stop reached. The verdict card says where, for example "WHAT IF 8 %: turn back at Altadena, no time for its work (red pillar) · ESTIMATE", or "turn back 640 m along the route, before Campo Marte". On the way home there is nothing to turn back from, so the search stops at the last leg. When the point is at a stop, the red beam wraps the stop's amber one and the stop's own tag line reads "TURN BACK HERE · with 8 %" in red (a second label there pushed the labels up over the main board's texts); elsewhere the red pillar carries its own "TURN BACK BY HERE" label. With Gale's four stops the point is at Altadena for a tank at 15 % and at 8 %. A point in the middle of a leg is tested with one far stop of no work at 8 % (183 m along the way out): there the red pillar is lower than the stops (45 % of their height), the other labels never move for it, and its own label slides sideways to the first place where it covers no other.
- **The visor says how far the point of no return is.** While walking the mission, the minutes-back card's last line reads "turn back in 200 m" (route meters from where the astronaut stands) until that point; past it TURN BACK NOW comes on as before and the line is the oxygen margin again.
- **Picked stops and the point of no return on the visor's and the wrist's maps.** A picked stop's mark is blue there too (the data's stay amber), and the point of no return is a red dot on the visor's map and a red pin on the wrist map. A mission with a picked stop is tested end to end: START MISSION and one jump per leg, the picked stop among them.
- **WHAT IF with hands.** A WHAT IF sign stands at the front-left corner of the table (RECENTER's mirror). Each press is one step of the stick's WHAT IF (100 -> 15 -> 8 -> 100 %), with the same rules; it hides with the table, and while a SUITS server sets the tank. Until now the WHAT IF needed the left stick's click: with hands, as at the Santa Cruz event, there was no way to show it. The controls card reads "WHAT IF · its sign, or left stick".
- **The stop card says the walk back, or that the stop is out of reach.** Its first line reads the meters and the RETURN layer's minutes back on foot ("716 m straight · 15 min back on foot"). When the plan does not fit its tank it says, in red, "Out of reach at 8 % · turn back at the red pillar" for a stop past the point of no return, or "At 8 %: no time for the work here · turn back" where the work no longer fits.
- **CLEAR asks first.** The first press turns it into SURE?; a second press within 3 s clears the picked stops, otherwise it goes back to CLEAR.
- **The time at Gale.** The app computes the time on Mars with NASA GISS's Mars24 algorithm (Allison and McEwen 2000): the local mean solar time at the Gale square, Curiosity's sol, and today's sunrise and sunset. The conditions card's daylight line, which was a REMS record of a Mars year ago, now reads today's, for example "Daylight: 06:28 to 18:21 · now 07:59 LMST (Mars24)". When a plan started now would not fit the daylight, the verdict's note says so: "Leaving now you are back at 19:50 LMST, after sunset (18:21)", or "Night at Gale now (02:00 LMST), daylight 06:28 to 18:21". The verdict itself stays the oxygen's, and a point of no return comes first on the note. Checked against five days of Curiosity's REMS records (sols 100, 1000, 3000, 4404 and 4995, from 2012 to 2026): same sol, Ls within 1.5°, sunrise and sunset within 1.2 min. The automatic test runs at a fixed Gale morning, so its results do not depend on when it runs. Sources in NOTICE section 8.
- **The visiting order of your stops.** A picked stop's card has EARLIER / LATER and "visit 2 of 5" in its last row (a picked stop has no risk note or photo for it): each press swaps it with the stop before or after, and the plan follows the new order. EARLIER is off for the first stop and LATER for the last; the data's stops do not move. The order is kept in `my_stops.json` with the stops' ids, so the picked stops come back in the order they were left, with their names.
- **Today's season.** The conditions card's season line, also a REMS record of a Mars year ago, now reads today's from Mars24: "Season: Ls 24°, southern autumn, the low-dust season (Mars24)" (southern seasons by Ls quadrant; Ls 180-360, around perihelion, is the dusty season).
- **Your stops are kept.** Every change writes `my_stops.json` in the app's data folder (the site's name, each stop's place and minutes). At start the stops of the same site come back, each through the same rules as a tap, before the first plan. A file of another site, or an unreadable one, gives none.
- **Planning off the main thread.** After a tap, a REMOVE, CLEAR or a minute changed, the plan is made on a worker thread with its own copy of the stops and shown when ready, so the headset does not stop for the ~200 ms a plan takes on the Quest. START MISSION waits for a plan still on its way. The WHAT IF and the start of the app still plan at once.
- **Optional link to a NASA SUITS telemetry server (off by default).** The SUITS challenge's test server, TSS (github.com/SUITS-Techteam), answers UDP requests on port 14141 with the suit's telemetry as JSON (EVA.json). If the file `suits.json` is in the app's data folder (`{"host":"192.168.1.20","port":14141,"eva":1}`), or `-suits host[:port]` is on the command line, Walkback asks every 2 s for that suit's primary oxygen (`oxy_pri_storage`). The plan then starts with that much oxygen instead of a full tank, at most once every 10 s and only when it moved 1 % or more; the verdict card reads "SUITS TSS suit 1 · primary O2 85 % at 12:03 UTC (simulated) · ESTIMATE", and the WHAT IF button is off. TSS's numbers come from its own simulation, not from a real suit, and the app's oxygen model is still plan B. Both shapes of EVA.json are read (2026: suits under `eva1` / `eva2`; 2027: one suit). During a mission the walk keeps the plan it started with (a new plan under it made the board and the visor disagree): a reading taken on Mars waits and is applied back at the table. Tested only against a stand-in server on the laptop, never against a running TSS.
